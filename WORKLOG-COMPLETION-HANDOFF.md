# Worklog Desk completion handoff

The user is a Business Central DevOps engineer who records Windows activity across browsers, VS Code, RDP, Outlook, and Teams. Worklog Desk already gathers evidence and supports assigning Jira issues, approving worklogs, and sending them to Tempo in 15-minute increments. The current problem is following through on submission. Build and pilot **Finish today’s worklog** before expanding capture.

## Evidence and product decisions

| What to test | Evidence | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A reminder when action is possible | In an Australian randomized trial among people with a history of late income reporting, one SMS before the deadline increased on-time reporting from 53.1% to 66.6%. A U.S. Treasury randomized trial found essentially no change after repeating a near-identical reporting email. The stakes, recipients, and cadence differ from daily worklogs. | Send one opt-in cue at a chosen time with a specific next action. Keep follow-up bounded. [Australian BETA trial](https://www.pmc.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/sms-timely-reminders_0.pdf) · [U.S. Treasury trial](https://oes.gsa.gov/assets/abstracts/2112F-SLFRF-abstract.pdf) |
| A concrete if–then plan | In an employer vaccination field experiment, a date-and-time planning prompt increased uptake by about 4 percentage points over the same reminder alone. This measured a one-off action. | Let the user set their own workdays and time. Example setup copy is “At 16:45 on workdays, I’ll review and submit my worklog.” [Milkman et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1103170108) |
| Progress the user can act on | Harkin et al. synthesized 138 randomized studies. Progress-monitoring interventions improved goal attainment on average, with standardized effect size 0.40 (95% CI 0.32–0.48) and heterogeneous results. The intervention class involved checking progress toward a goal. | Show submitted, ready, and still needs review. Use actual worklog state as the measure of progress. [Harkin et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/) |
| Fewer steps between cue and completion | A randomized administrative-form study increased FAFSA submissions with a streamlined process and filing assistance. Information alone did not improve outcomes. The study tested a bundle. | Open the relevant date and action directly, and let ready worklogs go through while difficult items wait. [Bettinger et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjs017) |

These are causal findings in adjacent settings. Their transfer to a recurring Jira/Tempo workflow is a hypothesis. Keep points, streaks, manager-facing progress, and billable-hour targets out of this pilot. Time totals are for reconciliation.

## First implementation

1. **Opt-in setup.** Choose workdays and a reminder time in the existing workday time zone. Allow skipping today for leave or a holiday. Keep a persistent private in-app summary reachable from the tray. Older unfinished dates go in one in-app list.
2. **Bounded cue.** One non-modal Windows notification for the date when there is actionable work. Use generic lock-screen-safe copy such as “Worklog Desk has items to review.” Show counts, titles, and issue keys only in the app. Support one explicit 30-minute snooze. As proposed pilot defaults, the first cue expires 60 minutes after the scheduled time, and all cues expire two hours after it or at local midnight, whichever comes first. A late start or unlock inside the first window can deliver the cue once. After expiry use the in-app summary. Persist the last notified date, skip, and snooze state. Keep next-morning notifications out of the pilot. Test restart, sleep, lock/unlock, clock changes, and both DST transitions. For an ambiguous local time use one occurrence, and for a nonexistent time use the next valid local time, subject to expiry.
3. **One click to the next action.** Open the correct date, including if an existing review window shows a different date. Open Worklogs when entries are ready to send or reconcile. Otherwise show drafts to approve or Activity filtered to unresolved rows. Keep an explicit switch between the two. Sending remains a separate, user-confirmed action. Preserve partial sends and existing 15-minute rounding and reconciliation.
4. **Say what is known.** A successful Tempo write or a reconciled matching receipt counts as verified submission. Existing explicit **Mark entered** counts as manual attestation and remains distinguishable. Approval, copy/export, a failed or unconfirmed write, and open corrections never count as verified submission. If a read or authentication failure prevents verification, show “Tempo status unknown” for affected items and preserve prior confirmed receipts. Use the current rules for substantive unassigned or uncertain spans, incidental switches, and idle gaps. Keep visible blockers open until assigned, classified, or otherwise resolved through the existing workflow. If capture is empty, show “No activity recorded” and offer an explicit “Nothing to log” where appropriate. A self-declared no-work day or leave is separate from submitted workdays. When outstanding work is cleared, use “No remaining items in Worklog Desk.” New activity can reopen the summary without sending a second unsolicited notification.

## Pilot and next features

- Collect five eligible workdays without the cue and ten with it. Before the baseline, choose a fixed evaluation cutoff in the workday time zone, such as 18:00, independent of the reminder schedule and expiry. Use the same cutoff in both periods. Treat the comparison as descriptive. If changes in workload or missing data make it hard to interpret, alternate baseline and reminder blocks only if the user wants to keep testing.
- Primary outcome is the share of actual workdays fully closed by that cutoff. Closure requires cleared substantive blockers and verified or explicitly attested submission of all required entries. Include days that had work but missed capture in the denominator. Use a short private check-in to distinguish those days from days off. Track verified and manually attested submissions separately. Record partial submissions, open blockers, unknown status, and explicit no-work days separately. Use **Needs review only** as the starting point for which activity states require a decision and have the planning board document any deliberate differences.
- Secondary measures are review opened within 15 minutes of a cue request issued to Windows, time to first submission and full closure, snoozed sessions still open at expiry, carried-over dates, corrections, and duplicate entries. A cue request does not establish that the user saw a notification. If Windows notifications are unavailable or silenced, the tray and in-app summary are the fallback. Use local aggregate or manual records for the pilot. A click indicates someone opened review. A receipt or explicit attestation indicates submission.
- If the cue gets the user into review but assignment stalls, build the collapsible right-side **Assign** pane next. Default to activity first, and let users choose a Jira ticket first in the same pane for known work. Keep the selection and target visible and confirm each batch. Add **paste Teams message link → find or create Jira issue** without importing the message body. Outlook Sent Items metadata can follow as opt-in attribution evidence. Preserve recorded duration as the source of timing. In the earlier Nov 19–23 aggregate review, Teams had 133.5 unassigned recorded minutes and Outlook had 18.2. That shows where context may help. It does not distinguish chats, calls, or internal work.

## Boards and acceptance

The research planning review averaged **9.15/10** after revision. The independent scores were 9.4 for follow-through evidence, 9.2 for progress and friction, 9.5 for desktop design and billing integrity, and 8.5 from Claude. The revised plan had no blockers. Claude’s remaining comments prompted the explicit pilot cutoff, cue issuance measurement, and Windows notification fallback above.

Use this handoff in a fresh session opened on the same checkout. There are pre-existing uncommitted Jira, Tempo, startup, UI, and test changes. Inspect `git status` and the diff before edits and preserve them. If using a separate checkout, first establish which changes it contains. The research session ran `dotnet run --project tests/WorklogDesk.Tests -c Release` and got **49/49**. Existing tests use synthetic/local fixtures and fakes. Worklog Desk runs as a Windows tray app. Start with `TrayApplication.cs`, `ReviewWindow.cs`, `ReviewWindow.Worklogs.cs`, `WorkStore.cs`, and `Dialogs.cs` under `src/WorklogDesk.Windows`. Reuse the current approved, entered, correction, and unresolved states through a shared structured summary. Avoid parsing display text or querying the store on every capture tick.

1. **Planning board before coding.** Ask at least three independent reviewers, including Claude if available, to review the same concrete plan for behavioral evidence and desktop UX, time-zone and notification lifecycle, and Tempo/data integrity. Have each give a 0–10 score, blockers, and concrete changes. Calculate the arithmetic mean. Proceed at **9.0/10 or above with no unresolved blocker**. Re-review the revised plan after material changes.
2. **Implementation verification.** Use an injected clock and synthetic store. Cover expiry, restart, lock/sleep/resume, DST, deduplication, snooze, skip, empty/manual-only days, stale selected dates, partial sends, failed or lost responses, copied/exported states, and work arriving after completion. Verify that reminder interactions cannot write to Jira or Tempo. Exercise the actual notification-to-review path on Windows 11 using synthetic data, including a lock/sleep/resume pass. Run the full suite.
3. **Independent code-review board before handoff.** Ask fresh reviewers, including Claude if available, to inspect the actual diff and test results. Cover UX/accessibility, scheduler and persistence, and billing correctness/privacy. Apply the same independent scoring and **9.0/10 mean with no unresolved blocker**. Review the reminder diff in the context of the existing changes and report findings against the correct change set. Fix findings and re-review the changed code. Report scores, tests, and anything that remains unverified. A review score measures readiness for a pilot. User behavior during the pilot measures whether it helped.
